Jamais Vu is an algorithmic composition for solo cello, dedicated to Matthias Lorenz, and created using a computer program. Its score exists in infinitely many versions as each execution of this program rewrites the music, generating a new unique score. Thus, every little detail in every version of this work has been logically created according to the composer's strict rules and yet these details are all unforeseen, making two scores generated by the same program look alike but never be the same.

In his The Psychology of Déjà Vu: Have I been here before?, Vernon M. Neppe defines jamais vu (French for 'never seen') as '[a]ny subjectively inappropriate impression of non-familiarity of the present despite numerous past exposures.' Likewise, even though the listener of this work may develop a sense of intimacy with this music, every new version will invariably create a sense of non-familiarity.
